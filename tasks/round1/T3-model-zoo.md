# T3 — Model zoo

**Depends on:** nothing, but pairs naturally with T2 (more features to feed).
**GPU:** light. TabPFN wants a GPU for inference; everything else is CPU.
**Owns:** `src/models/gbm.py`, `ridge.py`, `tabpfn.py` (new);
`src/models/__init__.py`; `requirements.txt`.

---

## Why this matters for the challenge

`REGISTRY` in `src/models/__init__.py` contains exactly one model:
`DecisionTree`, a single sklearn tree with `max_depth=5`.

That is a placeholder, not a baseline. `requirements.txt` contains no
gradient-boosting library at all. Every non-chemprop number this repo can
currently produce comes from one shallow tree.

Two consequences. First, we have no credible classical baseline to check the
D-MPNN against — if chemprop is underperforming a tuned LightGBM on ECFP4, we
would not currently know. Second, **T4 has nothing to ensemble.** Stacking needs
diverse base models, and the survey is unambiguous that ensembles are how
competitive submissions are built.

From `reports/PXR-SUMMARY.md` §3:

- Tree ensembles are the primary model in the bottom two thirds of the
  leaderboard and *ensemble members only* in the top decile. Their value here
  is as diverse contributors with OOF-learned weights, not as the final answer.
- ranga_31489 ran a clean ablation on one feature matrix: XGBoost
  (val RMSE 0.750) beat HistGradientBoosting (0.809) and random forest (~0.83).
  LightGBM and CatBoost are interchangeable with XGBoost in practice.
- Ridge contributes a smooth linear signal that complements nonlinear trees —
  cheap, and useful precisely because it fails differently.

**The highest-value item here is TabPFN, and it is nearly free for us.**
TabPFN and TabICL are tabular in-context learners: no gradient training, the
whole `(X_train, y_train)` is passed to a transformer at inference and its
pretrained prior over tabular datasets does the work. Unlike a tree, which
forgets its training labels at prediction time, TabPFN sees every training
label during every prediction — which is exactly what captures analog-series
patterns.

The survey's evidence: TabPFN was the dominant ensemble member in ldbc1999's
Sub 7 (ElasticNet coefficient 0.42); auP7s built an entire rank-27 pipeline on
TabICL with no GNN at all; and Ben Hicham et al. (2025) report TabPFN+CheMeleon
achieving up to 100% win rate on the MoleculeACE activity-cliff benchmark.

The pairing that makes it work is **TabPFN on CheMeleon embeddings**, and this
repo already computes and caches CheMeleon embeddings. The expensive half is
done.

## How it fits the current repo

- `src/models/base.py` defines `CYPModel` — a 23-line interface (`fit`,
  `predict`, `name`). `src/models/decision_tree.py` is the 39-line reference
  implementation; copy its shape.
- Register by appending to `REGISTRY` in `src/models/__init__.py`. The module
  docstring already documents this procedure. **Append only, never reorder** —
  this file is a shared surface.
- `evaluate-models` and `generate-results` resolve models through `REGISTRY`
  and features through `INPUT_REGISTRY`, so a registered model is immediately
  runnable against every featurizer T2 produces, on the pinned split, with no
  new CLI code.
- `src/models/utils.py::drop_nan_rows` handles the sparse-label problem —
  sklearn-style models cannot mask missing targets the way chemprop does, so
  each isoform trains on its own non-null subset. Use it.

## Scope

1. **LightGBM and XGBoost** behind the `CYPModel` interface. One module, two
   classes is fine. Light hyperparameter tuning only — these are ensemble
   contributors, not the centrepiece.
2. **Ridge** with standardized features. Trivial, but its value in the stack is
   that it is wrong in different places than the trees are.
3. **Random forest** as the survey's reference point, mostly to confirm the
   published ordering holds on our data.
4. **TabPFN on CheMeleon embeddings, PCA-compressed to ~200 dims.** This is the
   one to spend real effort on.

   **Heed the documented failure mode.** discoverybytes ruled TabPFN out of
   their final stack because they fed it CheMeleon embeddings generated by
   OOF-fold-trained models, which differ from all-data embeddings, and the
   resulting distribution shift degraded test performance. Our
   `representations/chemeleon.py` is a *fixed* pretrained encoder with no
   fine-tuning, so our embeddings are all-data by construction and this
   failure does not apply. Do not accidentally introduce it by fine-tuning the
   encoder per fold.

5. Benchmark everything on the pinned split, per isoform, against the chemprop
   numbers in `reports/head_search_analysis.md`.

## Tools

- `lightgbm`, `xgboost` — new dependencies, add to `requirements.txt`.
- `tabpfn` (PriorLabs) — new dependency. Check the input-dimensionality and
  sample-count limits of the current release before designing around it; the
  PCA-200 step exists to stay inside them.
- `scikit-learn` — already present. `Ridge`, `RandomForestRegressor`, `PCA`,
  `StandardScaler`.
- `evaluate-models --models <name> --input chemeleon` for benchmarking.

## Done when

- All models registered and benchmarked per isoform on the pinned split.
- TabPFN+CheMeleon specifically compared against the chemprop baseline, since
  that is the pairing the survey singles out.
- `requirements.txt` updated.
- A results table handed to T4 in the agreed OOF format — coordinate that
  format with whoever owns T4 *before* you start generating predictions, or the
  runs get repeated.
